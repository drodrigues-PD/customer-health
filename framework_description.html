Framework description for the Customer Health and Momentum Matrix

Executive Summary

This is the Customer Health and Momentum Matrix. It's a unified framework that brings together customer health signals that PagerDuty already has, spanning Product Intelligence, Data Science and Salesforce, into one place, giving us a more consistent and actionable view of account risk and opportunity.

On top of that, we added a Momentum model, calibrated against a retrospective of over 500 churned accounts, that tracks the direction those signals are heading. Together, health and momentum give us one shared lens instead of two disconnected reads on an account.

The primary purpose is to help sales and customer success teams identify risk earlier, understand what's actually driving account health, and act more strategically to improve renewals and reduce churn. At the same time, this also helps Executives to have better portfolio visibility and Managers to have better monitoring patterns and guide teams.

The core principle behind this framework is that health on its own isn't enough. A declining trend can hide behind health signals that still look good, and a recovering trend can hide behind health signals that still look weak. That's why we treat momentum as its own dimension, separate from health, so trend and current state are never blended into one number that hides one or the other.

The report covers 205 accounts across the customer base, representing roughly $56 million in ARR.

The dashboard shows, at a glance, which system each signal comes from. Health, Trajectory, and Sentiment come from PagerDuty's Data Science Model. ARR at Risk and CSM Notes come from Salesforce. Competitor Risk is a union of five equal-authority sources spanning the Product Intelligence Model, the Data Science Model, CSM Notes in Salesforce, and Gong call transcripts - any one of them firing carries the same weight and the same consequence, there is no primary or secondary source. Gong call detail is shown separately from the Sentiment score, since the score itself blends Gong calls with email and support tickets, and the Gong view is one input among several, not the full explanation for the number.

How Health, Momentum and Segmentation actually work

What this dashboard is - and isn't

This is not a new scoring engine. Health, sentiment, and competitor intelligence already exist as separate outputs elsewhere in PagerDuty's systems, produced by our own data science and product intelligence pipelines. This framework's job is to apply one consistent segmentation logic on top of those existing scores and give every account a single, comparable answer: which group it's in, and what playbook to run. Nothing here recomputes the underlying scores - it organizes them.

2. Trajectory & Momentum - which direction it's heading

Trajectory starts at 0. Eleven signals across four families, plus two smaller standalone modifiers, add or subtract points - this is the raw, natural, signal-only direction. Sentiment is one family of four, not the whole story. Momentum is a different, related thing: the effective value shown on each account card. Momentum equals Trajectory by default, but it is not only this points system - see the override box below.

Competitor signals - five equal-authority sources, one flag, one consequence
  1. Migration Risk Signal (Product Intelligence) - a quantitative pipeline tracking migration-stage indicators, like schedule mirroring or incident-history copying. Fires at any migration stage, including the earliest ("low signal") stage; every stage carries identical routing weight.
  2. API Watch, point solution (Data Science) - detects a competing tool active directly in this specific account.
  3. API Watch, account-wide (Data Science and/or Product Intelligence) - detects a qualifying signal anywhere in this account's PagerDuty customer family: this account's own row, or a related account sharing the same customer record. Rolls up both the point-solution signal above and the Migration Risk Signal above across the family, so it can fire from either model, or both - an account can show this signal with none of its own if a related account under the same customer record has one. Works only through this override; it does not subtract points from Trajectory (Section 2).
  4. CSM Notes (Customer Success) - a qualitative signal from CSM-logged activity: a named competitor at sufficient confidence and a non-coexist direction. Fires on its own, with no requirement that another source also fire.
  5. Gong Calls - a replacement-risk score of 4 or 5 on any call inside the account's live 90-day window. Also fires on its own.
Always Needs Attention, no matter what Health or Momentum say:
Any one of the five sources above firing, on its own, forces Needs Attention and forces Momentum to Negative. There is no tier and no "blocks Expand only" middle ground - every source carries identical authority, and when more than one fires for the same account, all of them are listed together, not just the first one detected.

All five sources carry equal authority - none is a tiebreaker or a mere corroborator for another, and CSM Notes and Gong Calls are not limited to only adding evidence to a quantitative source. A customer explicitly confirming they're running the competing tool alongside PagerDuty rather than replacing it (a COEXIST classification, per the CSM Notes direction rules), for example, suppresses that specific note from firing at all, the same confidence bar applies regardless of which way the evidence points. A single unconfirmed note is never enough to move the needle.
